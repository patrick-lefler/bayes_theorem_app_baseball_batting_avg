1. The Small-Sample Illusion

Every April, a batter gets 15 hits in their first 38 at-bats and posts a .395 batting average. Does anyone believe they are a true .395 hitter who will shatter league records?

The Coin Toss Analogy: If you flip a fair coin 10 times and get 7 heads (70%), you don't conclude the coin is rigged. You recognize that 10 flips is too small a sample to overcome your prior knowledge. In baseball, in-season batting averages are plagued by the exact same small-sample variance.

Empirical Bayes solves this by shrinking noisy early-season samples back toward the player's established career baseline.

2. The Beta Distribution as "Pseudo-At-Bats"

A hitter's true underlying probability of getting a hit (θ) is modeled as a continuous random variable using a Beta distribution:

θ ~ Beta(α₀, β₀)

In baseball, the parameters α₀ and β₀ represent real physical quantities:

  • Prior AVG (μ): The player's established career ability (e.g., .275).
  • Prior Weight (N₀): How much historical data anchors the prior (e.g., 450 at-bats).
  • α₀ (Pseudo-Hits): μ × N₀ = 0.275 × 450 ≈ 124
  • β₀ (Pseudo-Outs): (1 - μ) × N₀ = 0.725 × 450 ≈ 326

3. The Math: Beta-Binomial Conjugacy

Because the Beta distribution is the conjugate prior to the Binomial likelihood, updating our belief after logging new Hits ($H$) and Outs ($AB - H$) requires no numerical approximation—just simple addition:

α_current = α₀ + Total Hits
β_current = β₀ + (Total ABs - Total Hits)

The Bayes Estimate displayed on the dashboard is the mean of this updated posterior distribution:

E[θ | Data] = α_current / (α_current + β_current)

4. Shrinkage in Action Across 160 Games

The Bayes estimate is mathematically equivalent to a precision-weighted average between the prior career baseline and the raw season performance:

Bayes AVG = (1 - w) × Prior AVG + w × Raw Season AVG

where the weight ($w$) assigned to in-season evidence is:

w = Season ABs / (Season ABs + Prior Weight)
  • Game 10 (15 H / 38 AB, Raw .395): Season weight $w = \frac{38}{38 + 450} \approx 7.8\%$. The player's .395 hot streak receives only 7.8% weight, shrinking the Bayes estimate to .284.
  • Game 110 (165 H / 418 AB, Raw .395): Season weight $w = \frac{418}{418 + 450} \approx 48.2\%$. With over 400 at-bats logged, the season data now carries equal weight to the prior, pulling the Bayes estimate up to .333.

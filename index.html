1. The Mystery: The Fire Alarm Paradox

Security vendors proudly advertise their alarm tools as "95% accurate". Yet when analysts arrive at work, over 90% of alerts are false alarms. Why?

The Popcorn Analogy: Imagine a school fire alarm that catches 95% of real fires and only makes a mistake 1% of the time on normal days. Because actual school fires are extremely rare (maybe 1 day in 1,000), that 1% mistake happens across 999 normal days. Almost every time the alarm screams, someone simply burnt popcorn!

This is called the Base Rate Fallacy: when genuine bad events are rare, even a tiny false-alarm rate will overwhelm your team with noise.

2. What the 10,000-Dot Canvas Shows

The Fleet Activity grid is an icon array representing 10,000 events happening across a company network in a single hour:

  • Dark Slate Pixels (9,890+): Normal, innocent computer traffic that passed silently with no alarm.
  • Amber Pixels (~100): Harmless programs (like software patches or Spotify) that tripped the alarm by accident (False Alarms).
  • Flashing Pink Pixels (~10): Real hacker or ransomware intrusions caught by the sensor (True Alerts).

Notice how the amber dots outnumber the pink dots 10 to 1. That is why an initial alert only has an ~8% to 9% chance of being real!

3. The Math: Bayes' Odds Formulation

Instead of messy fractions, this app calculates Bayes' Theorem using Odds and Likelihood Ratios (LR):

Posterior Odds = Prior Odds × Likelihood Ratio (LR)

Step 1: The Base Rate (Prior Odds)
With a 0.10% breach rate (1 in 1,000), the starting odds are:

Prior Odds = 0.001 / 0.999 ≈ 0.001001 (1 : 999)

Step 2: The Initial Sensor Alert (LR = 95×)
With 95% sensitivity (TPR) and 1.0% false alarm rate (FPR):

LR = 0.95 / 0.01 = 95.0×
Updated Odds = 0.001001 × 95.0 = 0.0951 (Odds: 1 : 10.5)
Probability = 0.0951 / (1 + 0.0951) = 8.68%

Step 3: Sequential Clues (The Diagnostic Deck)
Every investigative clue you click simply multiplies the current odds:

  • LR > 1 (Incriminating): Multiplies odds upward (e.g., Host Process × 25.0).
  • LR < 1 (Exonerating): Multiplies odds downward (e.g., Vendor SSL × 0.024).

4. The Operational Decision Gradient

Real cybersecurity isn't about red or green stoplights; it's about cost and action thresholds:

  • Noise Floor (< 1%): Probability is negligible. The cost of an analyst checking the computer exceeds the risk. Auto-dismiss!
  • Information Demand (1% - 50%): Elevated signal, but unconfirmed. Do not disconnect the user yet; run quick forensic checks!
  • Defensive Intervention (50% - 85%): Parity crossed. The risk of uncontained ransomware spread now exceeds temporary user disruption. Quarantine the machine!
  • Active Incident (> 85%): Breach confirmed beyond reasonable doubt. Launch full incident response.
